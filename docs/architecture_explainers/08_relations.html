Spatial relations

The scene graph is a tree rooted at the floor. Each object gets exactly one parent — what it rests on, sits in or is part of — decided from 3D geometry alone, in the gravity-aligned world (z up, floor at z = 0). "Next to" is added as a side link.

on

B supports A when A's ground footprint (alpha shape) overlaps B's by more than 10% of A's, B's lowest point is below A's, and they touch: B's top (95th pct.) is within 10 cm below or 15 cm above A's base (5th pct.), or A's base is within 10 cm above B's base (a cup standing inside a box). Nearest wins. Base within 15 cm of the floor → on floor.

inside

After the tree is built: ≥ 95% of the child's points inside the parent's 3D convex hull, and the child's base more than 15 cm below the parent's top (a mouse lying on a desk is "on", not "inside").

belongs

Only for part nouns (handle, drawer, door, knob, button, lid, cover, wheel, cushion, armrest, backrest, leg, shelf, faucet, tap): attached to the object sharing the most points within 10 cm.

next to

Same parent, footprints at most 30 cm apart and overlapping ≤ 10%, height ranges overlapping ≥ 50% of the shorter one. A side link; the tree keeps one parent per object.

Try it: drag the objects

A side view (horizontal = footprint along one axis, vertical = height). Drag boxes to move them; drag a box's right edge to resize. The tree on the right is recomputed with the rules above. Footprints are 1D intervals here; the pipeline uses 2D alpha shapes.
