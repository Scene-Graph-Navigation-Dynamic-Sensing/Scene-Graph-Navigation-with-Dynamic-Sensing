Instance naming

NOVIC is a decoder trained to turn a CLIP image embedding into a noun from a 42,919-noun vocabulary. It is run on each object's averaged embedding — once when its track ends, again only if the object changed later — and returns a beam of 10 candidate nouns with probabilities. Stage 7 turns that beam into one readable name, or "unknown".

The steps

  1. Track-end name or final decode. Objects already named at their last track end keep that beam; the rest are decoded from the final averaged embedding.
  2. Everyday name. A NOVIC noun not in configs/vocab/indoor_objects.txt is replaced by the listed name that best fits the image: CLIP score of "a photo of a <name>" + 0.2 × text similarity to NOVIC's own word ("billfold" → wallet, "office furniture" → chair).
  3. Head noun. Modifiers are stripped ("brown wooden office chair" → "office chair").
  4. Confidence, pooled over synonyms. The top score plus every beam alternative whose text embedding is ≥ 0.8 alike — NOVIC spreads probability over synonyms.
  5. Weak name fallback. If the pooled confidence is below the floor, a confident track-end name from a track that backed ≥ 25% of the detections is used instead.
  6. Below the floor (0.10 on Replica, 0.15 for video): "unknown" (datasets) or dropped (video).
  7. Support surfaces (table tops from clean-up) take the first surface noun in their beam, or "table", whatever the score.

Why a name can be confident and still wrong

NOVIC names what the crop shows. On Replica, 73% of non-object nodes (wall pieces, multi-object nodes) pass the 0.15 name floor, against 64% of real objects — ceiling pieces were named "curtain" (office_0) and "lamp" (room_0), with "room divider" high in their beams. That is why non-objects have to be removed geometrically or by the detection checks, not by name confidence.
